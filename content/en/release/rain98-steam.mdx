## What Is Rain98?

Rain98 is a narrative-driven adventure built around two lonely young people sharing a strange, rain-soaked everyday life. The official description sets the tone bluntly: Tokyo, 1998. An endless rain. A girl who wants everything to end. Two lonely youths whose choices will change the world.

You play as the person living alongside **Reina Amehara**, voiced by Rio Tsuchiya, a girl who refers to herself as "Angolmois." She isn't a companion in the cheerful sidekick sense — she's the emotional center of a story about endings, and your job is to keep her company while the ritual she's chasing moves forward.

The game blends visual novel segments with point-and-click exploration, light simulation elements, and branching choices. It's not a horror game in the jump-scare sense, but "psychological suspense" is the developer's own framing, and the atmosphere leans deliberately unsettling.

## Gameplay: Reina, the Angel Passport, and the Ritual

The core loop is deceptively gentle. You talk with Reina, you take on whatever request she throws at you, and you occasionally leave the apartment to walk through a rainy, retro-styled Shibuya together. Doing something meaningful for her earns a sticker for her **Angel Passport**, and each sticker pushes the ritual to end the world one step further.

That's the central tension the game is built on. You're not stopping the apocalypse; you're participating in it, one small kindness at a time. The store page puts it beautifully — building enough shared past to ensure there's no future to come.

### What You'll Actually Do

- **Novel segments:** Dialogue-driven scenes inside the apartment that develop Reina's character and slowly reveal what she's hiding.
- **Exploration:** Walks through 1998 Shibuya, dense with retro props, signage, and turn-of-the-millennium atmosphere.
- **Requests:** Small tasks for Reina that convert into Angel Passport stickers and ritual progress.
- **Choices:** Branching decisions the store tags as "Choices Matter," with multiple endings confirmed as a feature.

The presentation is a selling point in its own right. The store page emphasizes refined graphics and high-end animation, with blurry blue lighting layered over lo-fi music and constant rain. It's designed to feel cozy first and unsettling second — comfort with a shadow moving across it.
