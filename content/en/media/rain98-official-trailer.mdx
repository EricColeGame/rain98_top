## Why 1998 Tokyo Is the Real Star

A period setting is a design shortcut and a design trap. It hands you instant texture, but it also invites nostalgia that can paper over thin mechanics. Rain98's choice of 1998 Tokyo is interesting because that specific year sits on a hinge: modern urban Japan, but before the smartphone flattened everyday life.

| Era element | Why it helps a suspense story |
|---|---|
| Pre-smartphone communication | Characters can be genuinely unreachable, which is hard to write today |
| Analog media and payphones | Physical objects become clues instead of props |
| Dial-up and early internet culture | Isolation with a flicker of connection |
| Dense public transit | Crowds that still feel lonely |
| Constant rain | Sound design and visual repetition that build dread cheaply |

None of that guarantees a good game. But it explains why the trailer's imagery lands harder than its taglines. A city where you cannot instantly text someone is a city where a misunderstanding can last for days — and misunderstandings are the engine of psychological suspense.

The couple framing also matters more than it first appears. Two protagonists means dialogue has to carry the emotional load, and it means the "key decisions" are relational rather than tactical. If Rain98 pulls that off, the 1998 setting stops being wallpaper and starts being the reason the story could only happen then.

### Three tips for judging the next trailer

1. **Count mechanics, not adjectives.** A trailer that says "every choice matters" is marketing; a trailer that shows two visibly different outcomes is evidence.
2. **Watch for gameplay footage mixed with cinematics.** If the two are indistinguishable, you have not yet seen the game.
3. **Check the date stamp on coverage.** Showcase news moves fast, and older articles may describe a build that no longer exists.

Community reports so far are cautiously positive on tone and more measured on depth — which is the normal shape of a strong demo and an unproven full game.
