## How the Rain98 OST Builds '90s Tokyo

### Lo-fi as a storytelling tool

Lo-fi music works because of what it leaves in. Tape hiss, vinyl crackle, room noise, slightly muffled highs — these are imperfections that read to the brain as *memory* rather than *recording*. That's exactly the effect a game about foggy recollections and unreliable perception needs.

Rain98 leans into this hard. The soundtrack's job isn't to swell during dramatic beats; it's to sit under everything like weather. When your character's memories start feeling off and Reina keeps holding onto a secret, the **Rain98 OST** doesn't need to change key to make you uneasy. It just needs to keep being pleasant while something feels wrong.

### Diegetic sound and the rain

The developer specifically pairs the music with the sound effects of the surrounding environment — the arcade beeps, the payphone rings, the rain against the window. In practice, that means the line between score and sound design blurs. A hum from the refrigerator can sit in the same frequency range as a synth pad. Rain becomes rhythm.

| Layer | What It Contributes | Emotional Effect |
|---|---|---|
| Lo-fi music bed | Warmth, nostalgia, continuity | Comfort and safety |
| Environmental effects | Place and time (arcades, phones, rain) | Immersion in '90s Tokyo |
| Ambient silence | Space between cues | Dread, uncertainty |
| Sudden diegetic sounds | Disruption of the bed | Anxiety, wrongness |

### The horror hiding in the comfort

This is the smartest thing about the **Rain98 OST** as a concept. Psycho-thrillers usually signal danger with dissonance. Rain98 instead signals danger by making comfort *too* reliable — a cozy apartment, a charming girl, gentle music — and then letting the environment quietly fail. The soundtrack is the baseline you measure the wrongness against.
