| Channel Type | Typical Purpose | Best Practice |

It also means the bar for behavior is higher — a single spammy post is far more visible in a 200-person conversation than in a 200,000-person one.

## Sharing Fanart: How #Rain98_fa Works

The studio promotes a dedicated fanart hashtag, **#Rain98_fa**, which is a small but meaningful signal: art is welcomed, not merely tolerated. If you draw, animate, or make edits, that tag is your best bet for being noticed — both inside the **Rain98 Discord** and on social platforms.

Here is how to make a fanart post work harder for you:

1. **Post in the right place.** Use the art channel in the server and the hashtag on social media; do not rely on general chat.
2. **Put the tag in the post itself, not a reply.** Hashtags buried in threads get missed.
3. **Include your handle on the image.** Reposts happen; a visible credit protects you.
4. **Write one line of context.** "First attempt at the character" invites conversation; a bare image often does not.
5. **Engage with other artists.** Reciprocity is the fastest route to visibility in any small fandom.

| Fanart Approach | Likely Outcome |
|---|---|
| Art + hashtag + short caption | Highest chance of shares and replies |
| Art with no tag, posted in general chat | Scrolls away within minutes |
| Art reposted from another artist | Reported; damages reputation |
| Art plus process notes or timelapse | Strong engagement, often reshared |

Community reports suggest the studio occasionally highlights fan creations, which is another reason to keep the tag consistent and your credits clear.

## Staying Updated Beyond the Rain98 Discord

A chat server is a great home base, but it is a terrible archive. Announcements scroll away, and if you take a two-week break you will come back to hundreds of unread messages. A smarter routine combines several channels, each with a different job.

| Source | Strength | Weakness |
|---|---|---|
| Rain98 Discord | Fastest news, direct dev interaction | Noisy; hard to search later |
| Official X account | Public, shareable, easy to skim | Algorithmic; posts can be missed |
| Store wishlist page | Release and availability alerts | Infrequent updates |
| Fanart hashtag | Community creativity and momentum | Not official news |

Wishlisting the game is the single lowest-effort thing you can do. It costs nothing, it takes ten seconds, and for independent studios it is one of the few visible signals of demand that actually influences how a project gets prioritized. If you are excited enough to join a **Rain98 Discord** server, you are excited enough to wishlist.

For press, brand, or collaboration inquiries, the studio lists a dedicated PR contact address rather than handling business through chat. Use that channel for anything professional — do not pitch a developer in general chat.

## Practical Tips for Getting Value From the Community

The difference between a good server experience and a frustrating one usually comes down to habits, not luck. These are the ones that consistently work, drawn from player experience across small studio communities.

- **Check announcements on a schedule.** Once or twice a week beats constant refreshing.
- **Use search before posting.** Duplicate questions get less patient answers over time.
- **Contribute something small.** A screenshot, a translation, a bug report with reproduction steps — contribution earns attention.
- **Keep expectations realistic.** Small teams ship on their own timeline; pressure campaigns rarely speed things up.
- **Be the person who welcomes newcomers.** Community reputation compounds.

### Avoiding common pitfalls

| Pitfall | Better Approach |
|---|---|
| Asking for a release date repeatedly | Check announcements; wait for official word |
| Treating chat as a support ticket system | Use the designated feedback channel |
| Sharing unverified screenshots | Link the original source or say nothing |
| Posting in the wrong language channel | Match the channel's stated language |
| Leaving all notifications on, then muting everything | Keep announcements on, mute chat |

One more thing worth saying plainly: a **Rain98 Discord** invite is an invitation into someone's workspace as much as a fan club. The studio opened the door deliberately. Treating that space with the same courtesy you would show a real room is what keeps it open.
