## What the Demo Establishes: The Setup Any Ending Must Pay Off

The premise is deceptively simple. The protagonist wakes in his own apartment in 1998 and finds a previous tenant, Reina, standing in front of a noose. Instead of a ghost story, the two lonely people form a pact: complete 100 activities to fill a sticker book, which acts as the trigger for a ritual meant to end the world.

Community reports and early preview coverage agree on the tone: the world is presented as broken in both 1998 and the present, and both leads believe nothing will change. That despair is the engine of the plot — and it is also the first thing any ending has to resolve.

| The ritual | Designed to end the world | The stated goal may not be the literal goal |

## The Sticker Book and the 100 Activities as a Countdown

Player experience with the demo suggests this repetition is intentional — it makes the mundane feel earned — but it also raises a question about pacing across 100 tasks.

## Atmosphere and Cinematography Are Doing Ending Work

The demo's presentation is not decoration. The first-person camera, limited animation, handheld-style movement, film grain, and fully voiced dialogue from Reina all push the same idea: you are watching something intimate and slightly wrong.

The contradictions are deliberate. Rain98 sells the warm serenity of a cold, rainy night — street lamps on puddles, breath in the air, quiet streets — while the characters plan an apocalypse. That gap between comfort and annihilation is exactly where a memorable ending lives.

| Craft element | What the demo does | Why it hints at the ending |
