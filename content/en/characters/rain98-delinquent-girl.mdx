You share a small, dim apartment with her in Tokyo at the turn of the millennium, and together you work on a strange ritual meant to save the world from destruction. She is voiced by Rio Tsuchiya.

### Why Players Call Her a Delinquent

The shorthand sticks for a few reasons:

- **She is expensive.** You spend your hard-earned money keeping her fed and, above all, happy.
- **She is volatile.** The game explicitly warns that upsetting her leads to terrible things happening.
- **She withholds information.** Reina holds onto a secret for the entire story.
- **She has no interest in being likable.** Her charm is described as dangerous, not wholesome.

In other words, the **Rain98 delinquent girl** reading is less about a criminal record and more about a personality that refuses to be managed — which is exactly what makes her memorable.

## Why the Delinquent Girl Archetype Fits a Psycho-Thriller

The delinquent-girl archetype usually signals rebellion against a world that has already written the character off. *Rain98* twists that: Reina isn't fighting society, she's waiting out the apocalypse in an apartment with you.

| Archetype Expectation | Rain98's Twist |
|---|---|
| Defiant against authority | Defiant against your attempts to understand her |
| Street-smart and independent | Dependent on your wallet, yet emotionally in control |
| Rough exterior, soft center | Soft moments that may or may not be genuine |
| Knows the city's underbelly | Walks Shibuya with you, but keeps her history locked |
| Rebel with a cause | Ritualist with a secret agenda |

That last row is where the **Rain98 delinquent girl** persona earns its keep. The game frames the ritual as something you both decide to do, which means her rebellion isn't against the ritual — it's against telling you the whole truth about it. As memories fog over and reality starts to feel unreliable, her evasiveness becomes the engine of the psycho-thriller portion of the story.

Community reports from preview coverage suggest the tension escalates gradually rather than through jump scares, with the horror coming from small inconsistencies in her behavior and the apartment itself. That tracks with the game's stated premise: Tokyo's rain washes the truth away.

## Keeping Her Happy: Money, Mood, and Consequences

The core loop is simple to state and stressful to execute. You earn money, you spend it on Reina, and you avoid making her upset. The **Rain98 delinquent girl** does not accept excuses.

| Priority | Action | Risk If Ignored |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Food | Keep her fed before anything else | Mood drop, sour dialogue |
| 2. Happiness | Spend on gifts and shared moments | "Terrible things" per the game's warning |
| 3. Ritual progress | Advance the secret ritual | Stalls the main narrative |
| 4. Your own curiosity | Ask about her past | May provoke her — or reveal the truth |

A few practical habits help:

- **Budget before you shop.** Set aside a cushion for food and mood spending before buying anything for yourself.
- **Read her tone, not just her words.** The writing telegraphs irritation early; catching it early is cheaper than fixing it later.
- **Don't hoard.** Money sitting in your pocket does nothing for the **Rain98 delinquent girl** except make her suspicious.
- **Save often.** Mood systems plus unreliable narration is a combination that rewards frequent saves.

Player experience shared around previews consistently mentions that Reina's mood is the real difficulty slider. If you are struggling, the fix is usually economic, not narrative.

## Practical Tips for Your First Playthrough

If you want to enjoy the **Rain98 delinquent girl** storyline without stumbling into the bad outcomes the game keeps teasing, start here.

1. **Treat money as a mood resource.** Every purchase is either relationship maintenance or progress. There is very little neutral spending.
2. **Prioritize her happiness over your curiosity.** Prying into her secret early is tempting; it is also the fastest way to make her upset.
3. **Track what she tells you.** Memory fog is a stated theme, which means the game may deliberately contradict itself. Notes help you spot when.
4. **Ask "is this real?" often.** The story explicitly asks whether what you see is real or your mind playing tricks. Assume at least some scenes are unreliable.
5. **Play in short sessions with good audio.** The lo-fi atmosphere is the point; rushing it flattens the thriller beats.
6. **Expect ambiguity.** The **Rain98 delinquent girl** is not designed to be fully solved on a first run.
